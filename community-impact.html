OUR COMMUNITY

How can land subsidence affect daily life?

When the ground becomes lower or settles unevenly, the effects can reach our homes, roads, and workplaces. Flooding can add to these difficulties.

Below are examples of how ground movement and flooding can affect a community. They describe possible effects, rather than confirmed incidents in specific Hagonoy barangays.

01

Homes and buildings

A home may develop uneven floors or damage when the ground beneath it settles at different rates. Lower land levels can also add to flooding concerns.

For families, these conditions may mean repairs, extra expenses, and difficulty entering or using parts of their homes.

Cracks and uneven floors can have different causes. A proper assessment is needed to find out what caused the damage.

02

Roads and transportation

Uneven roads and floodwater can make travel slower and more difficult. Some routes may become harder to use until conditions improve or repairs are completed.

This can affect students going to school, residents travelling to work, and people delivering goods around the community.

03

Work and livelihoods

When roads or workplaces are difficult to reach, people may have trouble getting to work, receiving supplies, or serving customers.

Small businesses and households that depend on daily earnings may find these interruptions especially difficult.

These possible effects show why community planning should consider people's sources of income.

04

Access to services

Flooded or damaged routes can make it harder to reach schools, health centers, markets, and other places people need to visit.

Residents may need to take a longer route or wait until travel becomes easier. Knowing local conditions can help families plan their trips.

Related background sources: USGS, Maclang et al. (2026), and Bulacan State University. The examples above explain possible everyday effects; they are not results of a household survey.

A LOCAL INITIATIVE

Working together in Hagonoy and Calumpit

In March 2026, Bulacan State University announced the Calumpit–Hagonoy Basin Project. The initiative brings together the university, local governments, and development partners.

It aims to address land subsidence, long-lasting flooding, and related community concerns. The project is one example of how different groups can work together on local environmental problems.

Source: Bulacan State University (2026) .
