COMMUNITY ACTIONS AND SOLUTIONS

What can we do as a community?

Addressing land subsidence takes research, careful planning, and cooperation. We can start by learning about the issue, using water responsibly, and sharing reliable information.

01

Community actions

Being informed helps us understand changes in our surroundings and describe concerns clearly.

  • Read information from reliable sources before sharing it with others.
  • Talk about what you have learned with classmates, friends, and family.
  • When you notice a concern, record the location, date, and what you actually saw.
  • Raise local concerns with the appropriate barangay or municipal office.

Describe the observation without assuming its cause. A crack, uneven floor, or flood alone does not confirm land subsidence.

02

Sustainable practices

Responsible water use is one way to support better management of our water resources.

  • Turn off taps when they are not needed.
  • Repair or report leaking taps and pipes.
  • Avoid wasting water during everyday activities.
  • Learn about local water sources and support groundwater management based on research.

These habits help conserve water. Reducing subsidence also requires attention to groundwater use, local conditions, and decisions made across the wider community.

Background on groundwater use and subsidence: USGS. The water-saving tips are suggested everyday actions.

03

Government and institutional efforts

Local governments, researchers, and other partners have a role in understanding the problem and planning a response.

In March 2026, Bulacan State University announced the Calumpit–Hagonoy Basin Project, which includes the municipalities of Calumpit and Hagonoy and development partners.

The project aims to address land subsidence, long-lasting flooding, and related community concerns. It is an example of cooperation on environmental problems that affect both municipalities.

Source: Bulacan State University (2026) .

RESEARCH AND PLANNING

Why are measurements important?

Ground movement can vary across an area. Measurements help researchers understand where changes are happening and how fast the ground is moving.

This information can guide groundwater management, land-use planning, and decisions about where further monitoring is needed.

Sources: USGS and Maclang et al. (2026) .
