CAUSES AND EFFECTS

Why does the ground sink?

Land subsidence can happen because of natural processes or human activities. Understanding its causes helps us see how changes underground can affect our surroundings.

What causes land subsidence?

Groundwater extraction and the settling of underground layers are two processes that can contribute to subsidence. Their effects depend on the conditions in each area.

01

Groundwater extraction

Groundwater is water stored in spaces between soil, sand, and rocks underground. People pump it out through wells for different uses.

When groundwater levels fall, some underground layers can become more tightly packed. This can cause the land above them to settle.

The amount of movement depends on the underground materials and changes in groundwater levels.

02

Natural compaction

Soil and sediment can slowly settle under their own weight. As the spaces between particles become smaller, the layers become more compact.

This natural process can lower the ground over time. Measurements are needed to understand how much it contributes to subsidence in a particular place.

Sources: NOAA and USGS.

What has been studied in Hagonoy?

Maclang and colleagues examined ground movement in Hagonoy and Calumpit. Their study looked at its relationship with groundwater extraction and the growth of built-up areas.

The research shows why local measurements matter. Ground movement can vary from one area to another, so the same explanation may not apply everywhere.

What can happen when the ground becomes lower?

Subsidence can affect more than the land itself. It can add to flooding concerns and place stress on structures, especially when the ground settles unevenly.

01

Greater flooding concerns

When land becomes lower compared with nearby water levels, it can become more exposed to flooding.

Subsidence is only one factor. Rainfall, tides, and drainage conditions also affect when and where flooding happens.

02

Damage to structures

When parts of the ground settle at different rates, buildings, roads, pipes, and other structures may experience stress or damage.

However, a crack or an uneven surface alone does not prove subsidence. Its cause needs to be properly assessed.

03

Challenges for community planning

Changes in ground levels need to be considered when planning roads, drainage, buildings, and water services.

Reliable measurements help communities make better decisions about land and water use.

Related sources: USGS, Maclang et al. (2026), and Bulacan State University.
