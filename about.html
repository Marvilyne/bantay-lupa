What is land subsidence?

Land subsidence happens when the ground settles or sinks. It can take place slowly over many years or happen suddenly, depending on what is happening below the surface.

Sources: NOAA and USGS.

Why does the ground sink?

Groundwater is water found in spaces between soil, sand, and rocks underground. When water is pumped out, some underground layers can become more tightly packed. This can cause the ground above them to settle.

Groundwater use is one possible cause. Natural settling and other processes can also lead to subsidence. The cause depends on the conditions in each area.

Sources: NOAA and USGS.

Is it the same as flooding?

No. Subsidence happens when the ground becomes lower. Flooding happens when water covers land that is normally dry.

They can happen in the same place, but they describe different things. Seeing floodwater does not tell us whether the ground has moved.

Ground measurements and further investigation are needed to understand what is happening.

Why does this matter to Hagonoy?

Land subsidence has been studied in Hagonoy and nearby Calumpit. Maclang and colleagues used satellite observations to examine ground movement from 2015 to the first quarter of 2021.

Their research looked at how ground movement was related to groundwater extraction and the growth of built-up areas.

Learning about these findings helps us understand why changes in land and water use deserve attention in our community.

Source: Maclang et al. (2026) .

What do the study dates mean?

The paper was published in 2026, but the ground observations discussed here cover 2015 to early 2021. The publication year and the observation period are different.

Does land subsidence always happen suddenly?

No. Some ground movement happens slowly and may be difficult to notice. In other cases, the ground can sink suddenly. It depends on the process causing the movement.

Does a crack mean the ground is sinking?

A crack alone is not enough to confirm subsidence. Cracks can have different causes, so measurements and a proper assessment are needed.

How do scientists measure ground movement?

Scientists use methods such as satellite radar, precise GPS measurements, and repeated land surveys. These help them compare ground levels over time.
